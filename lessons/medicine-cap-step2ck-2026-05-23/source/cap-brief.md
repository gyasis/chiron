# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) — Step 2 CK primer

Pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma. Community-acquired
pneumonia (CAP) refers to cases that arise outside hospital or long-term
care settings and presents within 48 hours of admission.

## Epidemiology
- US incidence ~5-6 cases per 1,000 adults/yr; >1 million hospitalisations.
- Highest in <2y and >65y; mortality ~5-10% in admitted patients.
- Streptococcus pneumoniae most common typical pathogen.

## Etiology / risk factors
- Typical organisms: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, Moraxella.
- Atypical: Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila, Legionella.
- Viral: influenza A/B, SARS-CoV-2, RSV.
- Risk: age, smoking, COPD, asthma, immunosuppression, recent viral URI.

## Pathophysiology
Alveolar inflammation, exudate, consolidation. Impaired gas exchange →
V/Q mismatch → hypoxemia. Systemic inflammatory response drives fever and
leukocytosis.

## Clinical features
- Acute fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea.
- Tachypnea, tachycardia. Crackles on auscultation. Egophony, dullness to
  percussion in consolidation.
- Atypical pneumonia: subacute, dry cough, headache, less prominent fever.

## Diagnostics
- CXR — lobar or interstitial infiltrate, classic confirmation.
- CBC (leukocytosis), CMP, blood cultures (if admitted), respiratory
  cultures, urinary antigens (Pneumococcus, Legionella).
- CURB-65 + PSI scores stratify severity → outpatient vs admission vs ICU.

## Differential diagnosis
- Acute bronchitis, asthma exacerbation, COPD exacerbation
- Pulmonary embolism (consider when low-grade fever or risk factors)
- Heart failure (BNP, JVD, gallop)
- Lung cancer (smokers, weight loss, recurrent post-obstructive PNA)

## Treatment
- Outpatient previously healthy: amoxicillin OR doxycycline OR macrolide.
- Outpatient with comorbidity: amoxicillin/clavulanate + macrolide, OR
  respiratory fluoroquinolone.
- Inpatient non-ICU: beta-lactam + macrolide, OR resp fluoroquinolone.
- Inpatient ICU: beta-lactam + macrolide OR + fluoroquinolone; cover MRSA
  / Pseudomonas if risk factors.
- Duration: 5 days minimum, longer if clinically unstable.

## Complications
- Parapneumonic effusion → empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis, septic shock
- ARDS
- Post-pneumonic chronic cough or fibrosis (rare)

## Prognosis
- Outpatient: <1% mortality.
- Hospitalised: 5-15% in-hospital mortality, higher in ICU.
- Most improve within 48-72 hours of appropriate antibiotics.
