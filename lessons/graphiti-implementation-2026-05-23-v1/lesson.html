Chapter 1 / 6

The two-axis insight

Why you care You're about to invest minutes-to-hours into deciding how to organise everything you save. Picking the wrong axis-mental-model means your queries return noise forever and you can't fix it without a data migration. Five minutes here saves you those queries.

Knowledge-graph systems collapse easily in your head: "it's where I save things." But Graphiti — like every memory backend worth using — has two independent coordinates. Mix them up and you'll either over-share data across contexts or starve a query you should have been able to answer.

Axis 1 — namespace

group_ids
"Which world"
  • Lets you ask: "answer only from this world."
  • You write once to a group; every query must declare which group(s).
  • Today: one value — developer_gyasisutton.

Axis 2 — domain

entity_types
"What shape"
  • Lets you ask: "what's my workflow for X" not "anything about X."
  • Five categories only. Hard-defined, not free-text.
  • This is where your real domain modelling lives.
Axis 1 is "which world." Axis 2 is "what shape."
Chapter 2 / 6

Axis 1 — group_ids: the namespace

Why you care Every query you'll ever make has to declare a namespace. If you don't understand what a group_id is, you'll either silently bleed personal data into work queries or vice versa.

A group_id is a data partition. Two episodes in two different groups are invisible to each other unless a query explicitly names both groups. Think tenant. Think Postgres schema. Think Notion workspace.

Chapter 3 / 6

Axis 2 — entity_types: the five categories

Why you care This is the lever most people miss. Searching with no entity_types filter means every preference, finding, and one-off pattern competes for relevance. Searching just for one type narrows the noise to "the kind of thing you actually need right now."

Inside one group_id, every episode carries a type that says what kind of knowledge it is. Your rules nail it down to exactly five — no improvisation, no twelfth category for "stuff."

Preference

How you want things done. Coding style, tool choices, interaction style.

"Preference: ANSI SQL for Snowflake+Databricks"

Workflow

Reusable multi-step processes. Setup patterns. Deployment recipes.

"Workflow: CREATE VIEW → GRANT SELECT → appears in Databricks"

ToolPattern

Proven command/API call sequences. The "do X, then Y" patterns.

"ToolPattern: Superset auth → API call → verify result"

Recipe

Documented fix patterns. Each one points at a file under ~/.claude/recipes/.

"Recipe: Superset params must be JSON string."

Finding

Atomic recurring facts: IDs, connection strings, auth tokens, project keys.

"Finding: Atlassian cloud ID is e29377f4-..."

Chapter 5 / 6

What's possible

Why you care You've been using one axis (entity_types) cleanly and ignoring the other (group_ids). Six moves below — pick the ones that match where you actually feel friction.

Move 1 — Per-domain namespacing

Spin up hh_dev_team as a second group_id. Migrate HH-only Workflows/Recipes/Findings there. Queries from inside HH work pass group_ids=["developer_gyasisutton", "hh_dev_team"]; queries from personal projects pass only the first. Personal projects no longer pick up "how to scaffold a Cube view" as relevant context.

Move 2 — Multi-tenant simulation

Use group_ids for roles: role_pm, role_oncall, role_engineer. Switch personas by switching the group_id. Same brain, different lens.

Move 3 — Entity-type-only queries for routine work

Build slash skills that bind to a single entity_type. /find-workflow only searches Workflows. /find-recipe only searches Recipes. Lower noise, faster convergence.

Move 4 — Promotion pipelines

Pair group_ids with the admission test to make promotion explicit. New patterns start in sio_pipeline (the holding tank), and only graduate to developer_gyasisutton when they pass Universal + Low-churn + Behavioral.

Move 5 — Audit + reconcile by axis

Audit just Workflows quarterly (they decay fastest), just Findings annually (they barely change), and just the smallest group_id whenever you suspect partition rot.

Move 6 — Sharing without leakage

If you ever ship a "Gyasi's setup" capability bundle to a teammate, you only export their relevant group_id. developer_gyasisutton stays private; hh_dev_team can ship if they're also on the HH stack.

Story 2 · Onboarding a new laptop

The portable identity, the local quirks

You set up a new MacBook. The cross-machine DNA — developer_gyasisutton — restores via ab compose --capability claude-config-maintainer. But the laptop also needs to know "which paths exist on THIS machine?" — your local Bitbucket clones, the Fusion venv, the ZENO_DIR drift workaround. Those go in onboarding_machine_macbook2026. They never pollute your other machine's queries.

Lever: group_ids as locality. Cost: one extra arg per query. Saves: ambient cross-machine confusion.

Story 3 · A six-month-old preference resurfaces

The drift detector

SIO flags "agent keeps suggesting gpt-4o as default." You query entity_types=["Preference"] — five rows — and spot the January Preference that says gpt-4o-mini is the floor. The drift is real. Enrich (not duplicate) the existing node with the 2026-05-08 incident reasoning. Graph stays under 30 episodes.

Lever: entity_types as audit lens. Cost: one targeted query. Saves: silent rule decay.

Story 4 · Sharing a capability with a teammate

Public DNA, private DNA

A teammate asks for your dbt-Cube setup. You don't want to hand over the contents of developer_gyasisutton — that's your interaction style, your home paths. But hh_dev_team is fair game. You export only that group. group_ids gave you a clean cut between "me" and "us."

Lever: group_ids as sharing boundary. Cost: maintaining one extra group. Saves: an awkward "let me sanitize this" review.

What NOT to do Don't shred your one group into eight tiny groups before queries actually need the cut. Partitioning is forever — un-partitioning is a manual data migration.
Today you have a 5-category, single-tenant graph. The leverage isn't to "add more" — it's to use the second axis you already have.
