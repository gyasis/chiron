## 1. Grammar pearl — `<strong>` the key term, `<em>` every Italian word cited, contrast/mistake warning

> **vorrei vs voglio.** Both mean "I want," but **vorrei** is the conditional tense
> ("I would like") — the polite form. **Voglio** is the present indicative ("I want")
> and sounds blunt in a service context. In restaurants, always use *vorrei*. Same
> principle as "Can I have…?" vs "Give me…!" in English.

Why it's good: opens by `<strong>`-defining the two terms, gives the rule, cites the
Italian in `<em>`, and ends on a common-mistake contrast the learner can feel. This is
the shape of a `pearls[].it` field (it carries the rich teaching text + markup; the
plain-English gloss goes in `pearls[].en`).

## 2. Grammar pearl — one word, many uses

> **Prego** has many uses: here it means "What can I get you?" / "Go ahead." You will
> also hear it as "You're welcome" after *grazie*. It is the most versatile one-word
> polite phrase in Italian.

## 3. Tip callout — dispel a false-friend, `<strong>` the term

> In Italian, **bar** means a café — not a place that serves only alcohol. Every Italian
> bar serves espresso, cappuccino, and cornetti (croissants). Alcohol is also available,
> but coffee is king.

## 4. Cultural note — `<em>`-cited Italian threaded through a real-world explanation

> In most Italian bars there are TWO prices: one if you drink standing at the counter
> (*al banco*), and a higher one if you sit at a table (*al tavolo*) — sometimes 30–50%
> more. The price list must be posted by law. If you want the local experience AND the
> cheaper price, stand at the bar.
