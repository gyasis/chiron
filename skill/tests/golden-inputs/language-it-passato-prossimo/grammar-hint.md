# Grammar Hint: Passato Prossimo

## What it is

The **passato prossimo** is Italian's most common past tense — used for completed actions in the recent or distant past. Despite the name ("near past"), it covers most narrative past-tense uses in modern spoken Italian.

## Construction

`[present-tense of essere OR avere]` + `[past participle of the main verb]`

Examples:
- `Ho mangiato` — I have eaten / I ate
- `Sono andato` — I have gone / I went

## Past participle formation

Regular endings:
- **-are** verbs → **-ato** (parlare → parlato)
- **-ere** verbs → **-uto** (credere → creduto)
- **-ire** verbs → **-ito** (dormire → dormito)

Irregular participles must be memorized: `fare → fatto`, `dire → detto`, `scrivere → scritto`, `leggere → letto`, `prendere → preso`, `mettere → messo`, `aprire → aperto`, `chiudere → chiuso`, `venire → venuto`, `essere → stato`, `vedere → visto`, `nascere → nato`.

## Choosing the auxiliary: essere vs avere

Use **avere** with:
- Most transitive verbs (verbs that take a direct object): `Ho letto il libro.`
- Many intransitive verbs of activity: `Ho dormito bene.`

Use **essere** with:
- Verbs of motion to/from a place: `andare, venire, partire, arrivare, uscire, tornare, entrare`
- Verbs of change of state: `nascere, morire, diventare, crescere, cadere`
- Reflexive verbs: `mi sono lavato, si è alzata`
- Stative verbs: `essere, stare, rimanere`

## Past participle agreement

- With **essere**: the participle agrees in gender and number with the **subject**.
  - `Marco è andat**o**.` / `Anna è andat**a**.` / `I ragazzi sono andat**i**.` / `Le ragazze sono andat**e**.`
- With **avere**: the participle is usually invariable (ends in -o).
  - `Maria ha mangiato la pizza.` (no agreement with "pizza")
- Exception: when a direct-object pronoun (`lo, la, li, le`) precedes the verb, the participle agrees with that pronoun.
  - `Le mele? Le ho mangiat**e**.` (agreement with `le`)

## Example sentences

1. `Ieri ho mangiato la pasta.` — Yesterday I ate pasta.
2. `Anna è arrivata alle otto.` — Anna arrived at eight.
3. `Abbiamo visto un bel film.` — We saw a nice film.
4. `Mi sono svegliato presto.` — I woke up early.
5. `Hanno scritto una lettera ai nonni.` — They wrote a letter to their grandparents.
6. `Sono nata a Roma nel 1995.` — I was born in Rome in 1995. (speaker is female)
7. `Le chiavi? Le ho messe sul tavolo.` — The keys? I put them on the table.

## Brief contrast: passato prossimo vs imperfetto

The **passato prossimo** describes a completed, bounded action ("I ate breakfast at 8"). The **imperfetto** describes ongoing, habitual, or background past states ("I used to eat breakfast at 8" / "I was eating when..."). A full chapter covers this elsewhere; for now, when in doubt and the action is finished and time-bounded, choose passato prossimo.
