Prima di tutto

The plus sign, doing a third job

Her + is back, and this time it marks a combination — two pieces that fuse, or a verb that needs a particular preposition.

+

Two words fusing into one. a + la  →  alla  — alla scrivania
su + il  →  sul  — sul divano

+

The preposition a verb insists on. non ricordo + di + prendere  — ricordare di + infinito
io + mi incontro  — the reflexive pronoun that belongs to the verb

L'articolo LO

Why it is never «il spazzolino»

The masculine article is normally il. But put an s followed by another consonant in front of it and it has to become lo — Italian will not stack those sounds. On the tape she gives you the rule directly: «perché la parola comincia con SP».

lo spazzolino
the toothbrush
sp- → lo. Not il spazzolino.
lo specchio
the mirror
lo stadio
the stadium
st- → lo.
lo psicologo
the psychologist
ps- counts. The test is s + consonant, not the letter s alone.
lo standard
the standard
Borrowed words obey it too: lo sport, lo staff.
lo zucchero
the sugar
z- as well — from prodotti con molto zucchero.
Perla di grammatica

Davanti a s + consonante l'articolo maschile è lo, non il: lo spazzolino, lo specchio, lo stadio, lo standard. Vale anche per ps- (lo psicologo), z- (lo zucchero), gn- e y-. Al plurale: gli — gli spazzolini, gli stadi.

Before s + consonant the masculine article is LO, not IL. The same holds for ps-, z-, gn- and y-. The plural for all of them is GLI.

Incontrarsi, occuparsi

When -si means each other

Not every reflexive points back at one person. Some are reciprocal: the action runs between people, and that is why the sign-offs you already use look reflexive.

incontrarsi
to meet (each other)
The -si is reciprocal here — one another, not oneself.
mi incontro con amici a Napoli
I meet up with friends in Naples
Keeps con.
noi ci incontriamo in centro a Napoli
we meet in the centre of Naples
in centro — no article.
occuparsi
to deal with, to look after
Takes di, always.
lei si occupa di appartamenti per i militari
she handles apartments for the military
si occupa DI — never a or con.
ci vediamo domani!
see you tomorrow!
Reciprocal — literally we see each other. From Day 1.
ci sentiamo domani!
we'll speak tomorrow!
sentirsi = to hear one another. Use it when the next contact is by phone or message.

Ricordare o ricordarsi

Two ways to forget

Both are correct, they just take different shapes — and one of them needs a preposition that English never asks for.

non ricordo il nome
I don't remember the name
ricordare + direct object — no pronoun.
non mi ricordo
I don't remember
ricordarsi, standing alone. More colloquial; both are right.
non ricordo niente
I don't remember anything
The double negative is required: non … niente.
non ricordo di prendere le medicine
I don't remember to take the medicine
Before a verb it needs di.
non capisco
I don't understand
non ti seguo
I'm not following you
Softer than non capisco — you're following the person.
seguimi!
follow me!
Imperative — the pronoun attaches, the opposite of the present.
Perla di grammatica

Ricordare regge un oggetto diretto: non ricordo il nome. Ricordarsi è riflessivo: non mi ricordo. Davanti a un verbo serve di: ricordare di prendere.

Ricordare takes a direct object. Ricordarsi is reflexive. Before a VERB you need DI.
